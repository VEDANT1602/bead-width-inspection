# Parameter choices and scope

Most quantities were assigned to expose the scheduling problem. Units are millimetres for width and instrument error, and segments for time. No shop specification, calibration file or fault distribution was supplied.

| Quantity | Reference value | Reason and alternatives evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| Build length and sample | 160 segments; 320 independent paired draws; seed 90217 | Original finite study. Segment counts give tangible per-build effects. Changing physical segment length changes their interpretation. |
| Tolerance | 0.050 mm | Defines the synthetic spec and the two original timing triggers. Pathological shifts 0.070–0.115 mm can exceed it. Also scored 0.035 and 0.065 mm, changing both labels and relevant triggers. Actual tolerance must come from a drawing. |
| Reset estimator smoothing | 0.45 weight on newest camera reading, initialized at zero | Responsive baseline, ≈1.16-segment half-life; chosen rather than fitted. Checked 0.20 and 0.70. |
| Reference allocation | Eight checks, blocks of 20; or 16, blocks of 10 | Fixed per-build cost. Periodic checks occur at the 10th or 5th segment. Triggers default to the final available segment and cannot borrow across blocks. No latency or per-check cost heterogeneity. |
| Background and camera | AR coefficient 0.7; innovation SD 0.005; independent camera SD 0.012 | Defines in-control variance. Grid varies persistence over 0.4/0.7/0.9 and camera noise over 0.006/0.012/0.024. Additional 0.0371 camera SD approximates the numerical scale of a published maximum RMSE, without matching its error law. Innovation SD was held fixed. |
| Reference noise | 0.006, independent additive Gaussian | A more precise source, accessed only on request. Also ran 0.003 and 0.012. No delayed or correlated reference error. |
| Fault onset and sign | Uniform integers 24–86; either sign equally likely | Ensures pre-onset and post-onset observations. One signed event per build. These are design choices, with no occurrence-rate estimate. |
| Fault waveform and scale | Uniform amplitude 0.070–0.115; persistent step; ramp over 24; 14-segment initial reversal pulse then opposite 0.75 amplitude at lag 42 | Creates distinct time scales and visible/hidden faults. Applied amplitude factors 0.65/1/1.35. The time constants and waveform parameters were not estimated or swept. |
| Cell weights | 3 shapes × 4 sources with equal weight | Stress scenarios rather than a production sample. Each source accounts for one quarter, including stable builds repeated across shapes. Report source-stratified effects for alternative mixtures. |
| Ambiguity allowance | 0.010 + 0.002 × segments since last check | Heuristic band around tolerance for the inspection decision. Starting width 0.005/0.010/0.020 and growth 0.001/0.002/0.004 were changed individually. |
| EWMA inspection chart | Weight 0.20; bilateral 3-SD limit | Conventional chart scale, adjusted for known AR correlation of the baseline width; the limit is not an ARL guarantee under selective inspections or bias resets. Tested weights 0.10/0.20/0.40; limits 2/3/4 SD. |
| Two-sided tabular CUSUM | Allowance k = 0.5, decision threshold h = 5 in units of raw in-control SD | Conventional starting settings for detecting a reference shift near 1 SD. Tested k = 0.25/0.5/1 and h = 3/5/7 individually. Chart statistics reset after checks; the budget limits only when a check can be taken. |
| Kalman state | Zero initial width/bias and covariance. Independent width and bias random-walk increment SDs 0.010 and 0.005 per segment | A working model for ongoing changes; it cannot encode the actual fault shapes. The ratio initially assigns more camera innovation to physical width. Variations: width SD 0.005/0.010/0.020; bias SD 0.0025/0.005/0.010. Instrument SDs match simulation settings. Real covariance misspecification remains untested. |
| Error cost and delay | False and missed segments initially equal; first-violation delay capped at 20 | Alternative miss cost evaluated by explicit break-even ratio. A side statistic counts until a simultaneous true violation and alarm; no-violation traces have no delay. These quantities do not estimate a part-level loss. |
